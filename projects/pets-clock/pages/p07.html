6. Inverse Relief Design

The relief is computed backwards from the picture: each ray of each wedge is given the shape that produces its row of cells.

6.1Walls and ramps

Each run of black starts with a crest just tall enough.

The crest sits on the inner edge of the run at z = H(1 − a/b). Its back is vertical, and on the lamp side it rises from the floor along a concave ramp six times its height. Where a neighbour leaves less room, the ramp shortens and steepens instead of covering another cell.

6.2Bright and half-lit blocks

The tilt of a top face sets its brightness.

Light arrives only 8–12° above the dial, so a small tilt changes a lot: E/E₀ = sin(e + θ)/sin e. Bright cells are solid wedges tilted 18° toward the lamp. Half-lit cells are tilted away by half the local elevation, high at the front and ending at exactly the height the shadow behind them needs.

6.3Lessons from the model

Two early solutions failed on inspection.

A first half-lit block ended in a 0.4 mm fin, too thin for wood; making the block itself the occluder removed it. Ramps three times the wall height outshone the eyes; a 6 : 1 ramp keeps them dimmer. The hour ticks were moved inside r = 56 mm, clear of every ramp.
