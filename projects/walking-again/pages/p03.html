2. Context

China retires nearly three million shared bikes a year and recycles them for metal, while fewer than one in a hundred older people use a walker.

2.1The surplus

About 12 million shared bikes are in service; nearly 3 million retire each year.1,2

Retired bikes are stripped by part type; frames and other metal parts are remelted into ingots and plastics are pelletised. One bike yields about 5 kg of aluminium, 2.6 kg of iron and 4.5 kg of plastic.2

Bikes also leave service after crashes or flooding, so every frame is inspected for cracks and dents before reuse; damaged frames go to recycling.

2.2The need

223.7 million people in China are 65 or older, yet 0.5% of them use a walker.3,5

More than 40 million older people fall at least once a year, and falls are the leading cause of injury death after 65.4 Among community-dwelling adults aged 65+, 16.3% in China used any mobility device against 22.2% in the US; walker use was 0.5% against 9.9%.5

2.3The gap

Recycling recovers the alloy but discards the work already done to shape it.

Melting a frame keeps the aluminium and loses the tubes, welds and heat treatment. Re-making the same shapes repeats that work, which Chapter 8 quantifies.
