Moiré Public Chair

- Interactive public seating design based on Moiré effect

Designer: Keqing Tantai

KEYWORDS

ABSTRACT

  • Moiré pattern
  • Phase modulation
  • Street furniture
  • Public seating
  • Stationary activity
  • Social interaction
  • Computational design
  • Laser-cut steel

Street vitality depends partly on stationary activities: people pausing, sitting and watching one another. Prompted by moiré flickering across an outdoor wire-mesh chair, this project designs that effect deliberately as a non-digital prompt for lingering. Two laser-cut steel chairs face each other, and each backrest carries one grating. A phase-modulated image appears only when a line of sight passes through both backrests, and it inverts every 31.5 mm the viewer walks. The cutting geometry was computed point by point and constrained for fabrication. The design hypothesises that this responsive image draws strangers into shared attention; on-site observation is proposed as future work.
