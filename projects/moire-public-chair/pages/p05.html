4. Form, Structure and Viewing Geometry

The chair's shape is set by two things at once: what a 4 mm steel sheet can carry, and where the observer's eye must be for the two gratings to line up.

4.1Form

One 4 mm steel sheet, folded three times into four faces.

Front leg, seat, rear leg and a 1.6 m back. Rounded bends (inner radius 30–70 mm) and 60° bevelled side edges stiffen the sheet without adding flanges; short flat feet sit at the two ground folds.

4.2Structure

The tall back is a propped cantilever.

A free 1.6 m plate would sway. A diagonal tube (Ø26.9 × 3.2) runs from the front leg through the seat to two vertical bars (Ø33.7 × 3.2, 500–1400 mm) welded behind the back at ±220 mm, just outside the grating. Each chair is anchored on its own by four exposed M10 security bolts in resin-anchored sleeves (Section 7).

4.3Viewing geometry

The rear window is the front window enlarged 5/3 about eye height.

Seen from an eye at 1600 mm, 3 m behind Chair A, Chair B is 2 m further away and appears at 3/5 size. Its grating window therefore grows from 216 × 590 to 360 × 930 mm, and its period from 12.6 to 21 mm. Because both backs recline 6° away from each other, B is not a plain scaled copy: every slot is back-calculated point by point (Section 5).

4.4Moving observer

Every 31.5 mm of sideways movement inverts the image.

Δx = pBD / d = 21 × 3000 / 2000. A slight sway of the head is enough to flip it, while the two eyes, 63 mm = 2Δx apart, always see the same phase, so the image stays sharp. It reads clearly from about 2.75 to 3.3 m behind Chair A.
