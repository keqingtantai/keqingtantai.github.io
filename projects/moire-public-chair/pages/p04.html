3. Principle and Route Selection

Three decisions turned an accidental effect into a buildable design: which moiré, where the two gratings sit, and how fine they can be.

3.1Which moiré

Only phase modulation shows any image with 4 mm cuts.

Criteria: chosen image · open area ≥ 50 % · features ≥ 4 mm

Line fringes

Two plain gratings. Robust and easy to cut, but only produces bands, never an image.

Shape moiré

Magnifies a repeated micro-motif. Motif must be far smaller than 4 mm features allow.

Frame animation

Barrier grid over interlaced frames. Slits of 1/n period: dark, fragile, and needs exact registration.

Phase modulation ✓

Rear slots bend by half a period inside the image. 50 % open, any image, inverts as the viewer moves.

3.2Where the layers sit

Two chairs 2 m apart give the fastest, sharpest response.

Δx = pBD / d, with pA = 12.6 mm and D = 3000 mm. A wide gap makes the image flip every 31.5 mm of walking, and 63 / Δx = 2 keeps both eyes in phase. (c) also needs one plate per chair, keeps each chair a single folded sheet, and places the image in the space between two seats.

3.3Grating period

12.6 / 21 mm balances detail against tolerance.

pB · D / (D + d) = pA  ⇒  21 × 3000 / 5000 = 12.6

Slot and bar must each be ≥ 4 mm in 4 mm steel, so pA ≥ 8 mm. Finer periods sharpen the image but narrow the distance band in which it stays clear and leave thin bars that warp in cutting. 12.6 / 21 mm was chosen after comparing all three in simulation.

Route chosen

Phase-modulated moiré, one grating per chair, chairs 2 m apart with a 0.6 m offset, periods 12.6 / 21 mm.
